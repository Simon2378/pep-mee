Lyophilised Peptide Vials: What Freeze-Dried Means and How to Store Them

Most research peptides arrive as a small amount of dry white powder in a sealed glass vial. That powder is lyophilised, meaning freeze-dried. This guide explains what that means, what to expect when you open the box, and how labs typically store these vials.

Published by Care Peptides on 11 Jul 2026 · Last reviewed 5 Nov 2026

Quick answer

Lyophilisation removes water from a frozen peptide solution under vacuum, leaving a dry powder or “cake”. Dry peptides are far more stable than dissolved ones, which is why they're shipped this way. Keep vials sealed, cold, dry and away from light, and check that the label, strength and lot number match your order and COA.

Key takeaways

  • Dry means stable: Removing water slows the chemical reactions that degrade peptides.
  • Looks vary: A solid cake, loose powder or thin film can all be normal.
  • Cold, dry, dark: That's the standard storage rule for sealed lyophilised vials.

What lyophilisation is

Freeze-drying happens in three stages: the peptide solution is frozen; then, under vacuum, the ice is turned straight into vapour without melting (primary drying, or sublimation); finally, gentle warming drives off the last bound moisture (secondary drying). What's left is a light, porous solid that holds a small amount of residual water.

Why peptides are supplied dry

In solution, peptides slowly break down through hydrolysis, oxidation and deamidation. Removing water slows these reactions dramatically, so a lyophilised peptide keeps far longer and survives shipping much better than a liquid would.

What the powder should look like

Usually white to off-white. It might be a neat solid cake, a loose powder, or a thin layer on the vial wall. Small amounts, such as 2mg, can be hard to see at all. Shipping can break up the cake, which is fine. Appearance alone isn't a quality test, but noticeable discolouration, visible liquid, or a damaged seal is worth raising with your supplier.

Anatomy of the vial

  • Glass vial: usually clear, small-volume pharmaceutical glass.
  • Rubber stopper: seals the vial.
  • Aluminium crimp seal: holds the stopper in place.
  • Flip-off cap: plastic cap protecting the stopper until use.
  • Label: product, amount per vial and, ideally, lot number.

On our site, a spec like 10mg*10Vials means 10mg per vial, 10 vials per pack. See the SKU guide.

General storage practice

  • Keep vials sealed until needed.
  • Store cold: refrigerated for short periods; many labs use −20°C for longer-term storage of lyophilised peptides.
  • Keep dry: let a cold vial reach room temperature before opening so moisture doesn't condense inside.
  • Protect from light and avoid repeated warming and cooling.

Always follow the storage conditions on the product label or COA where given.

Frequently asked questions

Is it normal if the powder is hard to see?

Yes. Low-milligram vials contain very little material, and it may sit as a thin film on the glass or under the stopper.

Does the mg on the label mean pure peptide?

It's the stated amount per vial. The powder also contains some water and counter-ions, so net peptide content is usually a bit lower. See the COA guide for details.

Can lyophilised peptides be shipped without refrigeration?

Dry peptides tolerate short periods at ambient temperature far better than solutions, which is why they're shipped dry. Store them cold once they arrive.
